A fair warning

The honeymoon is real. So is the bill.

The popular starter stack — Vercel + Supabase and friends — is genuinely great for getting started. Idea to working app in a weekend. For a prototype or a tool you use yourself, perfect.

But nobody mentions the catch: the same speed that makes month one feel like magic makes month four feel like a brick wall.

The pattern I keep seeing

  • All the logic in one file. Fast to write, impossible to grow.
  • Logic in three places at once. Frontend, database, edge functions — good luck finding where anything actually happens.
  • The browser drives your data updates. It writes the first half, the user closes the tab, the second half never runs — now your data's stuck halfway with no undo. A real backend does it all-or-nothing.
  • Security holes shipped as features. XSS, leaked keys, missing checks — the AI writes them in without blinking.
  • Hard limits at the worst moment. No long-running jobs: no real AI agents, no big uploads. You find out the day a customer needs one.
  • Spaghetti that compounds. Every shortcut makes the next one harder to avoid.
